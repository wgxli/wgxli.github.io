Notes on the Adelic Zeta Function

Published March 2, 2023｜4 minute read

This post motivates the complete zeta function ζA(z)=π−z/2 Γ(z/2) ζ(z)\zeta_{\mathbb{A}}(z) = \pi^{-z/2} \, \Gamma(z/2) \, \zeta(z)ζA​(z)=π−z/2Γ(z/2)ζ(z), explaining how it arises in a natural way by including contributions from all possible completions of Q\mathbb{Q}Q. The argument is from Andreas Knauf’s excellent lecture notes; I just fill in some details.

By Ostrowski's theorem, the only possible absolute values on Q\mathbb{Q}Q are the usual absolute value ∣⋅∣=∣⋅∣∞|\cdot| = |\cdot|_\infty∣⋅∣=∣⋅∣∞​ and the ppp-adic absolute value ∣⋅∣p|\cdot|_p∣⋅∣p​. The completion of Q\mathbb{Q}Q with respect to these absolute values give the real numbers R=Q∞\mathbb{R} = \mathbb{Q}_\inftyR=Q∞​ and the ppp-adic rationals Qp\mathbb{Q}_pQp​, respectively.

One performs Fourier analysis over R\mathbb{R}R by executing a change of basis to the eigenfunctions χu,∞:=exp(2πiux)\chi_{u, \infty} \defeq \mathrm{exp}(2\pi iux)χu,∞​:=exp(2πiux) of the translation group R/Z\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}R/Z. Here the frequency uuu takes values in Q∞\mathbb{Q}_\inftyQ∞​. An analogous basis exists for the ppp-adics; we may take χu,p:=exp(2πi[ux]p)\chi_{u, p} \defeq \mathrm{exp}(2\pi i [ux]_p)χu,p​:=exp(2πi[ux]p​), where u∈Qpu \in \mathbb{Q}_pu∈Qp​ and [ux]p[ux]_p[ux]p​ denotes the “fractional part.”

Fvf(u):=∫Qvf(x) χu,v(x) dxv.\mathcal{F}_v f(u) \defeq \int_{\mathbb{Q}_v} f(x) \, \chi_{u, v}(x) \, \dd{x}_v.Fv​f(u):=∫Qv​​f(x)χu,v​(x)dxv​.
